The kitchen, & the people in it.

Yim Wah Express is a Cantonese kitchen on the corner of Lensfield Road — the kind of place where the duck has been hanging since first light and the chef still answers the phone himself. We cook the food we know: the slow, hands-on cooking of Hong Kong's siu mei masters, plated for a Cambridge lunch hour.

Char siu is rubbed, hung, and lacquered through three glazes. The duck is salted and air-dried before it ever meets the oven. Beef ho fun is fired in a wok hot enough to leave the smoke on the noodle — what Cantonese cooks call wok hei, "the breath of the wok".

None of it is fancy. All of it is honest. The same dishes our families ate on a Sunday in Sham Shui Po; the same dishes we'd like you to eat with yours, this Sunday, in Cambridge.

港式燒味
No. 01

Char Siu叉燒

Marinated overnight in five-spice, hoisin, fermented bean curd and maltose. Roasted on the upper rack until the edges char and the glaze caramelises. Carved to order, served on rice.

No. 02

Roast Duck燒鴨

Five-spiced and stuffed, the cavity sewn, the skin scalded and air-dried. Roasted until the skin lacquers and crackles. Chopped on the bone, served with plum sauce.

No. 03

Crispy Pork Belly燒肉

Belly seasoned underneath, the rind pricked and salted. Roasted skin-side up until it puffs and shatters under the cleaver. Served with English mustard and caster sugar.

No. 04

Beef Ho Fun乾炒牛河

Flat rice noodles, marinated beef, bean sprouts, spring onion. Fired together in a wok hot enough to leave the smoke on the noodle — wok hei, the breath of the wok.

No. 05

Soy Chicken豉油雞

A whole bird poached in a master stock of soy, rock sugar, ginger and Shaoxing wine. Lifted out glossy, chopped to order. Served with ginger-spring-onion oil.

No. 06

Rice & Noodle Plates飯麵

Pick one meat, or pick two. Steamed jasmine rice or stir-fried noodles. Bok choy on the side. The lunchtime plate the way it's served in any siu mei shop in Mong Kok.
